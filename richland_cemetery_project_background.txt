RICHLAND CEMETERY PROJECT — "HOW WE MAKE THE SAUSAGE"

I'd like to take a little bit of time to share with you some information about the Richland Cemetery
project, where we're bringing together people, resources, expertise, and partnerships. These
following slides are going to show how the pieces fit together. It's basically a look behind the
scenes, and I'd like to share some information about how we're going to make this sausage.

Basically, people are going to be able to come to the cemetery and search categories. A map is going
to pop up, and they're going to be able to walk on that tour. Once they get to these different
locations, they would scan a QR code or NFC — something where they can scan a chip — and that chip
then delivers to them the data, so they discover the lives of the families and the folks of the
history living there. We want to go from this vision to that reality, and this is going to speak
about how we plan to get there.

WHY RICHLAND MATTERS

Richland Cemetery is important, so why are we doing this? The records, of course, were lost to fire.
A lot of things have been lost in time. Some things have just been uncovered — some were never known
in the past anyway. We want to bring all that up by using research tools, because every burial
represents a life and a story: some known, some forgotten, and some still waiting to be discovered.
And we want to share this with everyone.

THE TWO VISTAS

We have two different types of VISTAs that are going to help us do this. One is the data systems and
research VISTA, who builds data and research systems using a lot of different tools: drones, GPS,
digital cameras, smartphones, tablets, and computers. The other VISTA is concerned with partnerships
and volunteer systems. This person is building partnerships with the community and building a very
strong volunteer core that will enable this project to move forward. That volunteer core would be
the ongoing daily stewards of this project when the VISTAs leave.

BUILDING ON THE CITY'S GIS

The city of Greenville has an amazing GIS database currently. This is a map of Richland Cemetery —
the city's map — and it has a very good aerial overview where all the sections, blocks, and parcels
are clearly delineated. The city already knows where thousands of burials are located. What we
intend to do, to build upon that, is to dig into those little bitty squares and find out who these
folks are: to have a relational historical database where we reconstruct the lives of the folks that
are there.

In this case, for instance, Private John Williams — we'll look into his family. Who were his
parents, his siblings, his spouse, his children? Where did he live? What was his occupation? What
about his whole life story? There are historical records we can pull up and share: maybe birth
certificates, death certificates, marriage certificates, his call to service, and his registration
cards. It's really interesting, the type of data you can pull up.

One small veteran story we can pull up is right here. This is Sunflower Street, and this is Magnolia
Drive. Right here at Richland Terrace is a slew of veteran headstones. We had our group out to the
cemetery, and we were wondering: what's the story behind all these veterans buried there? Come to
find out, all of these guys served at Camp Sevier — which was over near this area, on Paris Mountain
— and all of them died of pneumonia at about the same time. So it's just an amazing amount of
stories that can be uncovered.

THE THREE TYPES OF CONSULTANTS

Initially, we think we're going to need consultant expertise from about three different types of
consultants.

One is a group that's really experienced with making databases. We want to build the database with
the end in mind, so we want to do an extreme amount of planning with this relational database up
front. We've already contacted a group called Taproot for database expertise. We've put in for a
60-minute consultation, and we want that consultation to lead to a scoping. Once we scope the
project, we want to bring in a team of volunteers that can help us think through all the key parts
of building this relational database — so by the time the VISTAs get on board, they'll have a
roadmap of what it is they're building and why these pieces fit together.

The second group of consultants we think will be really handy are GIS and data integration
consultants. The GIS is what the city already has, and the data integration is the stuff that we're
bringing. We need to integrate these two parts: all the information the city has with the stuff that
we're building.

The third type of consultants we think we need initially are drones. The city has, as we've seen,
this excellent 30,000-foot overview. If we're going to enable folks to walk to these locations, we
need very good high-resolution drone footage that will help visitors find individual graves —
high-resolution ground mapping so that, on the far right here, they'll know when they have arrived.

THE DATA BRIDGE

Again, this is a data bridge we're talking about. We're connecting the "where" that the city already
has — they have about 50 different items in this existing GIS data, and it's an extremely good
geographic foundation. We're building the "who": who these folks are, their lives, their families,
their stories.

This bridge is what we're really going to be concerned with connecting properly. The city has a lot
of unique identifiers, and we're going to have unique identifiers or codes or numbering systems, and
we need these numbers to match up. When we talk about John Williams, there might be several John
Williamses — John Williams the first, the second, the third; his family is buried at Richland. We
want to make sure we're matching the right John Williams up with the John Williams the city has at
that headstone. Obviously, some of the other work in this bridge is making sure the data is
integrated properly and that it's going to be one connected system.

ON-DEMAND TOURS

What's going to make this possible, again, is that anybody can come to the cemetery, do a search,
find what they're looking for, have a tour populated, go and scan those grave sites, and get to
discover the lives and history of the families buried there.

What we're really talking about are on-demand tours, where the same database we're building will use
the city's GIS data and produce many different thematic tours the community can experience — whether
it's the Veterans of Richland, or Families of Richland, or the Camp Sevier military veterans of
Richland, or Women of Richland, or Teachers of Richland. Whatever somebody wants to discover,
they're only going to be limited by their imagination with regard to the tour they can create on
demand.

THE LONG-TERM ECOSYSTEM

This will entail us building a very long-term ecosystem. The partnership VISTA — this is going to be
their main duty in year one — is to build this capacity to preserve and share Richland's history.
We're going to be connecting with city and community stewardship groups like the City of Greenville,
the Greenville GIS/IT Department, the Friends of Richland, and history, cemetery, and genealogy
groups; education and research groups; community, faith, and oral history groups; youth storytelling
groups; business, tourism, and community support groups; and media and design groups.

In year one, this is what the VISTAs are going to be building out. In year two, we will be
populating the entire database from our side — the "who," telling the story of the people buried at
Richland — and also getting the pilot tours on board and doing some experiences and programming at
Richland. And then in year three, refining it all. So we have one cemetery, one community, and
hundreds of stories that are going to unfold.

THE PHASED PATH

This is a phased planning path, from planning and design through building, connecting, and sharing.
We're going to scope out everything in step two, design it in step three, build it, then in step
four connect it, and in step five share it. And into posterity, the Friends of Richland — this rich
volunteer core that we're going to help develop — will be the ongoing stewards of this project.

The vision is one cemetery, one connected system, with hundreds of stories. It's going to build
something that will last and have a community impact.

And with that, this is where we are. We're really trying to build something that is one connected
system. We're building it in phases, from scoping it out all the way to the point of getting it
